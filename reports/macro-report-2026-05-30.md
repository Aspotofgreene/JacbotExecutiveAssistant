## 1. Inflation
**Current status:** **U.S.** — Headline CPI rose 3.8% YoY in April (the highest since May 2023), up from 3.3% in March, with core CPI at 2.8% (BLS, released May 12 2026). The Fed's preferred gauge, core PCE, rose to 3.3% YoY in April (headline PCE 3.8%), with the monthly core reading a softer 0.2% (CNBC/BEA, May 28 2026). **Canada** — CPI accelerated to 2.8% YoY in April from 2.4% in March, driven by a 28.6% surge in gasoline; ex-gasoline inflation was just 2.0%, and BoC core (median/trim) eased to ~2.1% (StatCan / CBC, May 19 2026).
**Interpretation:** Inflation is re-accelerating on both sides of the border, but the story is overwhelmingly an energy/oil shock from the Middle East conflict rather than broad-based demand pressure. The softer April monthly core PCE (0.2%) and Canada's tame ex-gasoline figure suggest the underlying trend is contained — and the late-May collapse in oil (see Section 6) should reverse much of the headline pressure into June.
**Market impact:** Elevated headline prints keep central banks cautious and support short-end yields, a mild headwind for rate-sensitive equities and bonds. But because the spike is oil-driven and oil has now fallen sharply, markets are looking through it — supporting the risk-on tone. Energy producers benefited on the way up; the reversal favors consumers and transports.
**Watch level:** 🟠 Elevated concern (headline re-accelerating, but oil-driven and likely peaking)

## 2. Unemployment
**Current status:** **U.S.** — April nonfarm payrolls rose 115,000 (above the 55K consensus but below March's 185K); the unemployment rate held at 4.3%; average hourly earnings +3.6% YoY (BLS, released May 8 2026). Weekly initial jobless claims were 215,000 for the week ending May 23, with the 4-week average at 209,000 — still historically low (DOL via Trading Economics, May 2026). **Canada** — Unemployment rose to 6.9% in April, a six-month high, as the economy shed 18,000 jobs (full-time -47K, part-time +29K); youth unemployment jumped to 14.3% (StatCan / CBC, released May 8 2026). The May U.S. jobs report is due June 5; the May Canada LFS is also pending.
**Interpretation:** The two labor markets are diverging. The U.S. remains resilient — low claims and a steady 4.3% rate — though hiring momentum is cooling. Canada's labor market is visibly deteriorating, with full-time job losses concentrated and tariff uncertainty weighing on hiring.
**Market impact:** U.S. labor strength underpins consumer spending and corporate revenue, supporting equities while giving the Fed room to stay on hold. Canada's weakness raises the case for BoC easing, pressuring CAD and helping rate-sensitive TSX segments (REITs, utilities) but signaling softer domestic demand for banks and consumer names.
**Watch level:** 🟢 U.S. low concern / 🟠 Canada elevated concern

## 3. Central Bank Policy
**Current status:** **Fed** — Held the target range at 3.50%–3.75% for a third straight meeting on April 28–29, in a contentious 8–4 vote (the most dissents since 1992); Miran wanted a cut, while three others opposed an easing bias (Federal Reserve, April 29 2026). Next meeting June 16–17. **BoC** — Held the overnight rate at 2.25% on April 29, where it has sat since October; the Bank flagged it could cut further on new U.S. tariffs or hike if oil stays elevated (Bank of Canada, April 29 2026). Next decision June 10.
**Interpretation:** Both central banks are in "wait-and-see" mode, caught between an oil-driven inflation impulse and softening growth (acute in Canada). The Fed's internal split shows a committee leaning toward eventual easing but unwilling to move while headline inflation is rising. With oil now falling and Canada in technical recession, the BoC's bias is tilting back toward cuts.
**Market impact:** "Higher-for-longer" persists for now, capping bond rallies. But a dovish Fed pivot later in 2026 is the bull case markets are increasingly pricing — fuel for the equity melt-up. Canada's easing path supports bonds and pressures CAD.
**Watch level:** 🟡 Moderate (on hold, data-dependent, easing bias building)

## 4. Earnings Growth
**Current status:** Q1 2026 S&P 500 blended earnings growth reached **27.7% YoY** as of May 8 — the highest since Q4 2021 — with 89% of companies reported and 84% beating EPS estimates (above the 5-yr average of 78%) (FactSet, May 8 2026). The index is also posting its strongest revenue growth in three years (FactSet).
**Interpretation:** This is exceptional, broad-based earnings strength, led by AI, semiconductors, cloud, and mega-cap platforms. Robust profit growth is the fundamental engine validating elevated index levels — earnings, not just multiple expansion, are doing real work.
**Market impact:** Strongly bullish for equities, particularly the AI/tech complex driving the rally. Strong earnings cushion valuation risk and explain the S&P 500's nine-week winning streak. Laggards remain in rate-sensitive cyclicals and parts of consumer discretionary.
**Watch level:** 🟢 Positive / low concern

## 5. Bond Yields
**Current status:** **U.S.** — The 10-year Treasury closed May 29 at 4.45% and the 2-year at 3.98%, leaving the 2s10s spread at +0.47% (a normal, upward-sloping curve) (Advisor Perspectives / YCharts, May 29 2026). **Canada** — The GoC 10-year ended the month near 3.45%, after spiking to a two-year high of ~3.74% on May 19 on the U.S. yield surge, then falling back as oil and inflation fears eased (Trading Economics, May 2026).
**Interpretation:** The yield curve has fully normalized (de-inverted) in the U.S. — historically a constructive late-cycle signal once recession risk fades. The mid-month spike and subsequent retreat in both markets tracked the oil-driven inflation scare and its reversal.
**Market impact:** A 10-year at 4.45% is elevated but stable, and the pullback from highs relieves pressure on housing, borrowers, and long-duration equities. A normal curve reduces the recession-signal anxiety that an inverted curve carries. Canadian yields falling toward 3.45% supports TSX rate-sensitives and bonds.
**Watch level:** 🟡 Moderate (elevated but stabilizing; curve normalized)

## 6. Oil Prices
**Current status:** Brent was around $94 and WTI fell ~2% to $87.2 on May 29 — the lowest in roughly six weeks (Fortune / Trading Economics, May 29 2026). For the month, WTI fell 16.2% and Brent ~17%, the largest monthly drop since 2020, after reports of a preliminary U.S.–Iran agreement to extend a ceasefire and ease Strait of Hormuz shipping restrictions (Trading Economics, May 2026).
**Interpretation:** This is the single most important market development of the week. The de-escalation of the Middle East conflict is unwinding the oil-driven inflation spike that had pushed CPI/PCE higher and kept central banks on hold. Lower oil eases inflation, transport costs, and growth drag simultaneously.
**Market impact:** Broadly disinflationary and risk-positive — a key driver of the equity rally and the easing in bond yields late in the month. Negative for energy producers and, at the margin, for the energy-heavy TSX and CAD; positive for consumers, airlines, transports, and most equities.
**Watch level:** 🟡 Moderate (sharp relief rally underway, but geopolitically fragile)

## 7. Valuation Metrics
**Current status:** The S&P 500 forward 12-month P/E was ~21.0–21.2 in early-to-mid May, above its 5-year average of 19.9 and 10-year average of 18.9 (FactSet, May 8 2026). The index closed May 29 at 7,580 (ninth straight weekly gain, longest since 2023) and the Nasdaq at 26,972, up ~8% on the month (TheStreet / Yahoo Finance, May 29 2026). The S&P/TSX Composite closed near 34,759 in late May (Trading Economics, May 2026); a specific TSX forward P/E was **not confirmed this week**.
**Interpretation:** U.S. valuations are stretched versus history, leaving less margin for error. However, the premium is partly justified by the 27.7% earnings growth — multiples are elevated but not divorced from fundamentals. The TSX trades at a structural discount to the S&P 500 given its value/resource tilt.
**Market impact:** Elevated multiples raise the risk of sharp pullbacks on any growth or AI-narrative disappointment, but rich valuations alone rarely end bull markets while earnings accelerate. The TSX's cheaper, value-oriented composition offers relative downside cushion.
**Watch level:** 🟠 Elevated concern (rich U.S. multiples, justified for now by earnings)

## 8. Recession Probability
**Current status:** **U.S.** — Q1 2026 real GDP grew 2.0% annualized, rebounding from 0.5% in Q4 2025, led by AI-related business investment (Deloitte/RSM via search, Q1 2026). Economist-surveyed 12-month recession odds sit around 30–33%, with the curve de-inverted; the Leading Economic Index still shows a -0.7% six-month decline as a caution flag (RSM / Conference Board, April–May 2026). **Canada** — StatCan reported Q1 GDP fell at a -0.1% annualized rate, a second consecutive quarterly contraction (Q4 2025 revised to -1.0%), meeting the technical-recession definition; weakness centered in resources, construction, and housing, with business investment down a fifth straight quarter on tariff uncertainty (StatCan / BNN Bloomberg, May 29 2026). Early estimates point to a sharp +0.4% rebound in April.
**Interpretation:** A clear divergence. The U.S. is in a resilient, AI-investment-led expansion with falling recession odds and a normalized curve. Canada is in a shallow, "trade-induced" technical recession that several economists (TD, Capital Economics) believe may already be ending as Q2 oil/gas activity rebounds.
**Market impact:** U.S. resilience supports the equity rally and corporate earnings. Canada's recession pressures CAD, domestic cyclicals, and banks, and strengthens the case for BoC cuts — though a rebound narrative limits the damage and could set up a recovery trade.
**Watch level:** 🟢 U.S. low/moderate concern / 🔴 Canada high concern (technical recession confirmed)

## 🌍 Global Watch
- **Europe (ECB / BoE):** ECB held the deposit rate at 2.0% on April 30, warning the Middle East conflict would lift near-term inflation; next decision June 11 (ECB, April 30 2026). The BoE held at 3.75%, with inflation now expected to peak at 3.0–3.5% in Q2/Q3 on higher energy prices (CNBC/RLAM, April 30 2026). Both are firmly on hold; the oil reversal is a relief for both.
- **Asia (BoJ / China):** The BoJ held its policy rate at 0.75% on April 27–28, cutting FY2026 growth to 0.5% and raising core inflation to 2.8% on the Iran-war oil spike; markets see further hikes (toward 1.25%) by year-end (CNBC, April 28 2026). China detail was limited this week, with Japan-China diplomatic tensions cited as a drag on external demand (not confirmed beyond that).
- **Geopolitics & commodities:** The dominant global driver is the reported preliminary U.S.–Iran ceasefire extension and potential Strait of Hormuz reopening, which triggered oil's ~16–17% monthly collapse (largest since 2020) and a broad disinflationary, risk-on impulse across global markets (Trading Economics, May 29 2026). The situation remains fragile and reversible.
